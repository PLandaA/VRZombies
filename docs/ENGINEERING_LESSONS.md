# Engineering Lessons (the bugs worth remembering)

Each entry is a bug that cost real time, what it turned out to be, and the rule that came out of it. Newest at the bottom.

- **AutoHand layers are collision rules, not labels.** Scenery placed on `Hand`/`Grabbable`/`HandPlayer` inherits AutoHand's runtime ignore-matrix and becomes walk-through despite a perfect collider. 46 props were on those layers; a layer audit plus a NavMesh rebake from `Default`-only colliders fixed both players and zombies.
- **Read the tracking rig in the right phase.** AutoHand moves camera and hand targets in `LateUpdate`; anything sampling them in `Update` sees a frame-old pose, and with physics stepping 0/1/2 times per frame that lag fluctuates into visible judder. Solvers must run after that write (VRIK ships at execution order 9998 -- we drive it explicitly).
- **Trust runtime geometry only.** Hand-model axes measured in edit mode were 49° off from the assembled runtime hand; every offset derived from them inherited the error. Live measurement (and finally, eyes in the headset) settled it.
- **`RaycastNonAlloc` buffers overflow silently.** A downward floor ray crossing a held rifle's collider cloud filled its 8-hit buffer with hand/weapon hits, dropped the ground, and fell back to a poisoned reference -- the body popped in sync with every step. Masking layers, widening the buffer and remembering the last good floor closed it.
- **A held weapon "vibrating" is a framerate bug, not a weapon bug.** AutoHand disables `Rigidbody` interpolation on hands, held objects and the player body and instead keeps `fixedDeltaTime` locked to the frame time (one physics step per frame). Below its 50 Hz floor -- the arena ran at 30-36 fps in the editor with a headset -- frames get 1 or 2 steps alternately and the joint-chained rifle hitches against the reprojected camera while walking; the lobby at 72 fps never showed it. A per-frame step histogram (`PhysicsStepMonitor`, editor-only) made the correlation undeniable, and the fix was the GPU budget above, not anything on the rifle. Measure with the Scene View closed: it renders the arena a second time (3-6ms) and blinds the GPU counter.
- **Terrain detail knobs that look alive but aren't.** `DetailPrototype.density` is ignored in `InstanceCountMode` (only `CoverageMode` reads it), and `useInstancing` set through the API on a `Grass`-mode prototype produces draw calls that draw nothing -- the inspector silently switches to `VertexLit` when you tick the box, the API does not. Both cost an afternoon of measuring the wrong thing.
- **`NetworkTransform` replicates local coordinates -- always.** Verified in the Fusion 2.0.10 IL: `CopyToBuffer` reads `localPosition`/`localRotation` and `CopyToEngine` writes them; `SyncParent` only decides whether the parent itself travels. AutoHand's `parentOnGrab` parents a grabbed object to `hand.transform.parent` (the tracking container), so while a partner held a magazine or grenade they sent coordinates relative to their play space, and everyone else rendered the object near the arena's world origin, following the partner's arm. (The avatar was never affected: its NetworkTransforms live in the same hierarchy on every client.)
- **AutoHand's `PlacePoint` has an opinion about that fix.** Simply turning `parentOnGrab` off broke magazine insertion: `PlacePoint.CanPlace` refuses objects without `parentOnGrab` when the place point belongs to another grabbable (the rifle's magazine well). The final fix keeps the flag -- AutoHand decides with it -- and `KeepWorldParentWhileHeld` undoes the actual parenting in `OnGrabEvent`, which AutoHand raises at the end of the same call that parents the object, so no network tick ever sees the parented state.
- **Snap turn already moves most of what you'd "fix".** The hands are children of the tracking container, and so is anything grabbed with `parentOnGrab`; `RotateAround` on the container turns them all, and AutoHand re-seats the right hand on its grab point. A helper that rotated hands and held bodies again left hand + weapon 30° past the controllers, and the hand follower dragged them back over the next frames: a whip after every turn. The helper now only moves held objects that live outside the container, by the container's measured delta.
- **Two motors on one object shake.** AutoHand's `ignoreWeight` adds a `WeightlessFollower` that drives the held object toward the controller, while the grab joint pulls it toward the physical hand. On a long rifle that was a violent oscillation on the host; turning `ignoreWeight` back off fixed it.
- **Never judge hand feel in a slow editor.** An editor-only helper relaxed AutoHand's physics-step floor to 1/30 s so walking would not hitch at 36 fps; at 28-33 ms per step the grab joint is under-damped and held objects oscillate. Logic and networking are fine to test with the editor as one client (make the *build* the master), but the feel of held objects has to be judged in builds at the headset's frame rate.
- **A tuned pipeline is part of its tuning.** The avatar's tick layer (`HardwareRig.OnInput` -> `CharacterInputData` -> `GetInput` -> pose in `FixedUpdateNetwork`, alongside the per-frame pose) looks like a Host Mode leftover in Shared Mode, since that input never leaves the client. Removing it as "cleanup" made the arms shake, because the smoothing had been tuned with it in place; it was restored byte-for-byte from git. Refactors of hand-tuned systems need a recorded baseline and a headset A/B, not just a green compile.
- **Targets that hang from what you move next.** The avatar's three IK targets (head and both hands) are children of the avatar body, and the rig placed them in world space and *then* moved, rotated and scaled the body, dragging them along by that frame's body motion. Standing still that is zero, which is why it survived months of tuning; walking, the head target ran ahead of the camera by speed / framerate (about 3 cm at 2 m/s and 72 fps) and VRIK pulled the neck into view. A frame-by-frame probe showed the head target's error equal to the body's per-frame displacement to the hundredth of a centimetre, and placing the body first made it constant. Two earlier measurements had missed it (one moved the camera in a way AutoHand undid every frame, the other walked so unevenly that the errors averaged out): aggregate numbers were not enough, per-frame ones were.
- **Filter in the right space.** A tiny exponential filter takes the buzz out of physics hands before they drive the arm IK. It ran in world space, so it also "smoothed" the player's own locomotion and snap turns: standing still the arm matched the controller exactly, walking it jittered about 0.8 cm per frame against it, and a sudden player displacement left the arm metres behind for a moment. The same filter in the player's tracking-container space leaves locomotion out of it: 0.00 cm per frame while walking. Together with the previous fix, this removed the slight shoulder and arm jitter the avatar had "always" had.
- **A state that enables a button is set when the work is done, not when it starts.** `EnterLobby` marked the session as *browsing* before awaiting Photon's directory join, so *Create Lobby* was clickable while the runner was still joining. In the editor that window is milliseconds; on a Quest over WiFi it is seconds, and a click inside it started the game on a half-joined runner, failed, and reloaded the lobby -- whose fresh manager cleared the error message before anyone could read it. Players learned to "press it a few times". The state now stays *Connecting* (buttons off, "Connecting..." shown) until the join result is Ok. The general form: latency the editor hides is latency the device will show, so gate UI on completed operations, never on started ones.
- **Never re-aim tracked eyes.** The first death spectator computed "where the eyes should be" every frame and moved the tracking rig so the *current* camera position landed there. That subtracts the player's own head motion: lean forward and the world slides back with the follow filter's lag (a third of a second), the textbook vestibular mismatch. A rig that carries a headset may only ever be *translated by what the followed thing did* and *rotated about the eyes*; the head's offset inside it belongs to the tracking system. The same design also defined "behind the partner" by their live facing, so every turn of their head orbited the spectator sideways -- continuous artificial motion in a game where the partner turns constantly.
- **One solve per frame, and no loops through the solver.** The arms still trembled while aiming, with or without the rifle. A probe with the hands perfectly still showed the avatar's shoulder bone moving 12-17 mm per frame, jumping 36 mm and back: VRIK alternating between two solutions. Two causes, one of them hiding behind a confident comment. First, `UpdateSolverExternal` only makes VRIK skip its own `LateUpdate` solve until the next `FixedUpdate` clears that flag, so on every frame with a physics step (all of them in the headset) VRIK solved twice, with the tick's stale targets and then with the fresh ones; combined with "rotate chest by hands" that is a period-two oscillation. The VRIK component now stays disabled and `NetworkRig.SolveIK` drives the solver once, after the targets are written. Second, the elbow hints were computed from the live shoulder bone, which VRIK moves according to those same hints: a feedback loop. They now use the shoulder's rest position in body space. Standing still the shoulder went from 12.5 to 0.03 mm per frame; with 3 mm of hand tremor, from 17 to 0.3.
- **A Rigidbody spawned by Fusion still sits where the prefab was.** The dispenser's magazines always ended up at one exact spot in the arena, whatever the spawn marker said. Not a push and not a slope: the spot was the prefab asset's own root position, saved from a scene instance. `Runner.Spawn(prefab, pos, rot)` moves the Transform, but with `Physics.autoSyncTransforms` off and Fusion calling `Physics.Simulate`, nothing tells PhysX, and the first step writes the *body's* stale pose back over the transform. Grenades never showed it because the belt writes their Rigidbody pose every frame, zombies because a kinematic body does not write back. A dynamic body you spawn loose needs its pose set explicitly. Found by spawning one in the empty lobby and watching it jump to the same coordinates.
- **A NavMesh agent that can "walk" everything walks into things.** The project's humanoid agent was baked with a 0.5 m height, a 55° slope and a 2 m step, over the entire 256 m terrain. Hillsides and the rocks ringing the arena counted as floor, so a zombie could path straight up a 50° slope and scrape along it looking stuck. Bake only the play volume, with an agent shaped like the creature (1.8 m, 35°, 0.4 m). Also: `CalculateTriangulation` returns detail-mesh seams at tile borders as near-vertical triangles, so "steep triangles" is not evidence of walkable slopes.
- **Edit nothing while Play Mode runs.** A script edit during a hosted test session recompiled under the running game; the domain reload left AutoHand's hands with null references and the console in a `NullReferenceException` loop. Nothing was damaged, but the session was lost. Tools that touch code must be used from Edit Mode.
- **A quality level the platform excludes is not the default, whatever the index says.** The "Quest" level was created by duplicating the PC level and inherited its *excluded platforms* list -- Android included. Unity kept it as the Android default by index and silently fell back to the only level left, "Mobile" (render scale 0.8, no MSAA, HDR on). Every APK up to 2026-10-07 shipped that way, and the first "72 fps at 1.4" figure was measured on it. The tell was one build-log line: `1 URP assets included in build: Mobile_RPAsset`. Read that line on every release build; and when a level is cloned, open its platform checkboxes.
- **"72 fps" is only a number if it was measured with OVR Metrics in the heaviest scene.** The Meta Horizon Store review rejected the first submission for running below 60 fps, and it was right: with the real Quest path (render scale 1.4, MSAA 4x, post-processing, main-light shadows) a Quest 3S showed **30-32 fps with the GPU at 95% and the CPU under 20%** in a six-zombie wave. The earlier "72/72" figure came from compositor log lines read in lighter moments; it was never a measurement of the arena under load. The fix was done one change per measurement, on a Quest 3S with OVR Metrics, same wave each time: render scale 1.4 -> 1.0: **50-56 fps** (the 1.96x pixel count was the biggest cost); post-processing off on the Quest renderer: **59-72** (Bloom's 1.1 threshold never triggers without HDR, so it cost a full-screen pass for nothing visible); main-light shadows off: **68-73** (a 0.32-intensity moon cast shadows nobody could see while 386 of 394 renderers redrew into the shadow map); fixed foveated rendering at level 1.0: **70-72 fps, GPU 80-85%**. Grass was not a lever: `TerrainQualityProfile` already runs Android at 10 m / 0.25 density. The reviewer tests on real hardware with the profiler open; so should every release.
- **Renamed audio files are silent bugs.** Three looping tracks went quiet months after being wired: the ambience pack had been reorganised outside Unity (13 files became 11, three renamed), and every scene reference to a removed file silently became null -- the arena's calm music layer, the lobby music and two of the random ambience slots. No error, no warning, just a game that only had music when the intense layer rose with the horde. A missing reference shows as `objectReferenceInstanceIDValue != 0` with a null value; a scan for that across both scenes is now part of the release check. Rename and move assets inside Unity's Project window only.
- **Animator culling hides a sliding-feet bug behind a camera that is not yours.** After game over the retreating zombies glided away with frozen legs even though the walk blend was correct (Speed 0.17, the clip advancing). The Animator was on *Cull Update Transforms* and Unity flagged the models as not visible while the dead player watched them -- the game-over view is not the camera Unity uses for that decision. Bones stopped being written exactly on the frames `Renderer.isVisible` was false (foot travel 17.8 cm then flat). *Always Animate* fixed it (foot travel 104 cm in the same 4 s); six animators always on are nothing against a CPU at 30%.
- **Two headsets that cannot see each other's rooms are usually in two Photon regions.** `PhotonAppSettings.FixedRegion` was empty, so every client picked its own "best region" by measured ping; rooms only exist inside one region, so a partner landing on `usw` never saw a room created on `us`, same city or not. Single-PC tests (the editor as the dummy partner) can never reproduce it. Fixed region `us` (2026-10-07); the Photon dashboard's region whitelist does the same server-side for APKs already installed. The second thing to check in that situation is the APK version: the room directory is named `VRZ-<version>`, so two builds never share rooms by design.
- **Design remote effects for the bystander, not the shooter.** The partner heard every shot but "never saw the tracer": the remote tracer was the same 70 ms, 12 mm flick as the local one, and ended 30 m into the dark because proxies did not know the hit point. The shooter catches it because it coincides with their own recoil and gaze; a bystander looking elsewhere does not. Now the owner replicates the impact point alongside the shot tick (12 bytes, written in the same frame so proxies read both in one change) and the remote tracer runs 180 ms and 1.5x wider. Rule of thumb: if an effect is only perceptible to the person who caused it, it is not replicated in any way that matters.
- **A NavMesh agent can refuse a complete path and tell you nothing.** A zombie stood still, facing the player, forever: `isOnNavMesh` true, `CalculatePath` returning a *complete* five-corner path, and `SetPath` returning false every half second -- the agent's internal mesh location had gone stale. `Warp` onto its own position repaired it instantly. The code around it had three silent `return`s for "not on the mesh", which is how a transient agent hiccup became a permanent statue. Now: the agent is re-registered on spawn unconditionally, a refused `SetPath` warps and retries, chasing without a path for 2 s warps again, and the one case that cannot be repaired logs in every build. Also found on the way: paths were sampled from the player's *head* with a 2.5 m radius, which fails wherever the walkable floor sits more than ~0.8 m above the mesh (seventeen spots in the arena); targets are now projected to the feet first.
